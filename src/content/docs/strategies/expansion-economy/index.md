This guide covers **Gods & Kings**. Expansion should preserve your ability to grow, research and defend. City count is not a measure of development quality: evaluate resources, useful population, location and ongoing costs. Use the actual game panels for final figures.

## Check four conditions before founding another city

| Check | A reason to proceed | A reason to pause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resources | A new luxury, strategic resource or strong usable land | Mostly duplicates and weak early tiles |
| Happiness | Room for settlement and growth, or a luxury about to connect | Existing unhappiness without a timely remedy |
| Finances | Enough income for extra defense, buildings and connections | A nearly expired deal is the only thing balancing the budget |
| Security | Settler, Worker and city have support | An isolated site beside a strong rival needs a separate army |

The conditions need not be perfect, but each gap needs a remedy. “An existing Worker will connect the new luxury immediately” is a plan. “More cities must make us stronger” is not.

## Give citizens valuable work before specializing cities

Food supports population, citizens work tiles and contribute base science, and production turns those yields into buildings and units. Compare net food, completion times and current assignments. Moving a citizen from a farm to a mine may help a deadline while stopping growth. Revisit the assignment when the project ends.

Give each city a main priority:

- **Population and science core:** secure food, build the research chain and employ Scientists when the food budget permits.
- **Production core:** support enough population to work mines and build important units or projects.
- **Resource or frontier city:** connect resources, secure its position and establish basic yields before copying every advanced building.

These are priorities, not restrictions. A science city can build emergency defenders and return to development later. Specialists also occupy citizens; an empty slot is not automatically a mistake.

## Happiness: watch sources that will disappear

Cities and population create unhappiness, while annexation can add an extra burden. Luxuries, buildings and policies help, with actual effects depending on settings and state. Easy-difficulty happiness is not a reliable budget for harder games.

A second copy of the same luxury is generally a trading asset, rather than another full source of luxury happiness. Consider exchanging spare copies for types you lack. Before selling your last copy, check the resulting happiness, imported luxuries nearing expiry and whether war could disrupt supply.

When happiness falls, identify the cause: growth, settlement, conquest or an expired agreement. Pause the next expansion, connect available luxuries and build suitable facilities such as a [Colosseum](/database/buildings/colosseum/). Adjust growth selectively rather than automatically shutting down every city. Protect productive core cities while fixing the actual shortage.

## Gold: inspect expenses before adding buildings

Separate durable income from temporary deals. Unit, building and road maintenance continue every turn. A lump-sum sale adds cash but does not repair an ongoing deficit.

A [Market](/database/buildings/market/) supplies base gold and a percentage bonus, making it useful in cities with a gold base to improve. If the problem is a long, unfinished road, several Markets may arrive too late. First pause new unproductive spending, inspect existing assignments and deals, then place economic buildings where they will help.

Keep an emergency reserve for a specific potential need, such as an important upgrade or defensive purchase. Look up its current price before deciding how much expansion you can afford.

## Road example: does the connection cover new maintenance?

Without relevant modifiers, a connected non-capital city's base gold income is:

**Capital population × 0.15 + connected city population × 1.1 − 1.**

An ordinary road has base maintenance of 1 gold per turn for each road tile you must maintain. With a population-6 capital and population-4 second city, the connection yields **4.3 gold per turn**. If completing it adds five maintained road tiles, income minus new road maintenance is **−0.7 gold per turn**. Military mobility may still justify the road, but this example is not yet profitable from connection income alone.

This is a base-rule calculation. Policies, abilities, ownership, existing roads and alternative connections change the result. Count only genuinely additional maintenance when several cities share a route. Construction can incur costs before the completed connection pays income, so organize Workers to finish necessary segments efficiently.

These are city connections. Do not import a caravan production plan from another expansion into this ruleset's economy.

## Conquered cities need a purpose before annexation

Inspect resistance, happiness, location and long-term usefulness. Puppets reduce some direct management demands but still affect the empire. Annexing gives production control while potentially increasing unhappiness and policy costs. Avoid annexing a low-production city in resistance merely to control its queue sooner.

A [Courthouse](/database/buildings/courthouse/) removes extra annexation unhappiness and has its own maintenance. It does not remove normal empire-wide population and city unhappiness. For a future production base, plan the Courthouse and infrastructure after resistance. Reassess a burdensome city using the available handling options and your victory objective; original capitals require special care in [domination](/strategies/domination-victory/).

## Worked decision: should a three-city empire settle a fourth?

Suppose happiness is barely positive, income is small and a Settler can claim a new luxury. If its improvement technology is distant and the site needs a long road plus more troops, prepare technology, Workers and defense first.

If a rival is about to claim a critical site, early settlement can still be justified. Pause other commitments, retain defensive cash and accept the temporary efficiency cost deliberately. The question is what you are willing to pay for that land.

Every few turns, revisit expiring trades, happiness, income, idle Workers, unproductive roads and citizen assignments. Stabilize existing cities before extending the empire into another deficit or exposed frontier.
