## Choose a victory route

Enable the relevant victory in game settings. These routes share development and defensive foundations, but select a main objective as the game develops and keep checking its bottleneck.

| Route | Endpoint in this ruleset | Main decisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Science](/strategies/science-victory/) | Apollo and assembly in the capital: 3 Boosters, Cockpit, Engine and Stasis Chamber | Population, research buildings, specialists, aluminum and production |
| [Culture](/strategies/culture-victory/) | Complete 5 policy branches and build Utopia | Policy costs, branch compatibility, culture buildings and final production |
| [Domination](/strategies/domination-victory/) | In an ordinary settled game, control all major original capitals, including your own | Targets, technology windows, consolidation and overseas operations |
| [Diplomacy](/strategies/diplomatic-victory/) | Someone builds the UN, then you win a qualifying diplomatic vote | City-state relationships, influence margins, gold and vote checks |

Time victory also compares scores at the configured maximum turn when applicable. It is not a separate route in this first collection. Religion is a support system; the default ruleset has no separate religious victory.

## Three reading paths

1. **Your first complete match:** first game → first 50 turns → expansion and economy → science victory. Learn stable development and reach a clear endpoint.
2. **Repeated battlefield losses:** combat and sieges → postwar economic management → domination victory. Preserve the army before practicing a chain of campaigns.
3. **A peaceful alternative:** expansion and economy → culture or diplomacy → religion when useful. Their policy and resource priorities differ; you need not execute both plans at once.

## Evidence and scope

Victory conditions, incompatible policies, building effects, road income and unit restrictions were checked against the pinned game source and rules. Build orders, suggested city counts and priorities are editorial advice. Worked situations are constructed decision exercises, not recorded matches or promises of a fixed victory turn or high-difficulty win rate.

The opening guide uses Standard speed for illustrative turn ranges. Other speeds, maps, civilizations, difficulties and mods change the pace. Consult the [database](/database/) and [cost explanation](/mechanics/) for reference, and [About](/about/) for source boundaries.

The collection now covers general play, four civilizations and four practical mechanics topics. More civilizations, documented Emperor+ matches, dedicated naval and air guides, and multiplayer strategy remain future work. A teaching example without a save or replay is not presented as an actual match record.
