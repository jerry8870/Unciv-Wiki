This guide covers conventional land combat in **Gods & Kings**. Abilities, promotions and terrain change available actions, so consult the combat preview before acting. The suggested formation is a learning exercise, not a guaranteed answer to every army or technology gap.

## Define what the fight must achieve

Defense protects cities, resources and experienced units. A siege removes the defenders and delivers a capture-capable unit into the city. You need not leave a secure line to chase a wounded enemy when your purpose is simply to stop an invasion. A capital campaign need not capture every remote settlement on the way.

Before fighting, compare the main units' technology, approaches enemies can use and the space available for retreat. Total military strength cannot replace local analysis: an army on another continent cannot stop this turn's attack.

## Build a formation from roles

| Role | Main job | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Melee front line | Screen ranged units and capture cities | Repeatedly attacking a strong city until no capturer survives |
| Ranged units | Focus fire and damage cities from protected positions | Moving before checking sight and firing lines |
| Siege units | Concentrate damage against cities | Deploying without protection or forgetting setup |
| Mobile units | Scout, intercept reinforcements and exploit flanks | Chasing one target into several enemy attacks |
| Reserve | Replace a wounded front-line unit or close a gap | Committing everyone at once |

For a Classical-era exercise, start with two front-line units, several bow units and siege support when appropriate. Adjust numbers to frontage, enemy strength and finances. Another reserve can be more useful than extra melee units that cannot fit through a mountain pass.

## Defense: invite the enemy into concentrated fire

Place blockers on useful defensive terrain or near the city. Put ranged units where they can fire without being easily reached. Keep retreat tiles free of Workers and other obstructions. Check movement, combat modifiers and line of sight separately around rivers, hills and forests. Two tiles of geometric distance does not guarantee a shot.

Prioritize enemies that can threaten your valuable units next turn: a melee unit near an Archer or a dangerous siege engine, for example. A safe kill usually reduces retaliation more effectively than lightly damaging several units. Decline a chase that exposes the whole formation.

Each attack changes the next preview. Use safe ranged damage, then decide whether a melee finishing attack is still worth the risk. Do not assume all outcomes in advance.

## Win the field battle before committing siege weapons

Sending siege units beneath a city while its field army is intact gives the enemy easy targets. Reduce reinforcements from favorable terrain, scout behind the city and confirm that vulnerable units cannot be reached unexpectedly. Rotate and heal before advancing if necessary.

A [Catapult](/database/units/catapult/) has an attacking bonus against cities, but must set up for ranged fire and receives no defensive terrain bonus. Plan a protected deployment tile before it arrives. Whether movement, setup and fire fit in one turn depends on remaining movement and the unit's rules. Siege weapons need screening and vision; they are not simple replacements for bow units.

## A practical siege-turn sequence

1. **Check threats and retreat space.** Reassess new reinforcements and wounded units. Repair or withdraw a broken formation first.
2. **Remove immediate counterattack threats.** Use safe attacks against units near your line instead of focusing exclusively on the city.
3. **Fire with weapons already in position.** Recheck city health after each attack.
4. **Inspect the capture preview.** Ordinary ranged bombardment cannot capture the city. You need a suitable melee attack from a unit that can capture.
5. **Prepare for the turn after capture.** The occupier is often wounded and the city can be counterattacked. Keep support nearby.

Adapt the order to the battlefield. Preserve a capture-capable unit that can survive the consequences. If your only capturer is nearly dead, delaying may be better than taking the city and immediately losing it.

## Rotation preserves experienced forces

Withdraw wounded units while a safe route remains and replace them with reserves. Healing depends on location, actions and abilities; do not assume every unit heals normally after moving. Inspect the actual state before ending the turn.

Before upgrades, confirm technology, resources, gold and location requirements. Ideally upgrade before the next offensive instead of waiting beneath enemy fire. Highly promoted units are difficult to replace, and protecting one may matter more than eliminating another ordinary enemy.

## Worked decision: a weak city you should not capture yet

In this constructed situation, bow fire has left the city with little health. Your only capture-capable warrior is badly wounded, and another enemy melee unit appears behind the city. Charging in might capture it but invite an immediate recapture.

Attack the reinforcement first and bring another front-line unit forward. If that cannot happen safely, maintain protected fire or withdraw the warrior to heal, then capture when you can hold. City health is only one condition; your survivability and the enemy's response matter too. This example assumes no fixed damage rolls or win probability.

## After the battle, decide whether to continue

Inspect resistance, happiness, resources and routes before choosing city management. Seek a pause or peace when technology falls behind, losses outpace replacements or a second frontier becomes threatened. War does not obligate you to fund an endless attrition campaign.
