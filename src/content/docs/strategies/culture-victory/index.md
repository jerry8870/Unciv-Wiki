In **Gods & Kings, Cultural victory requires five completed policy branches and the Utopia Project**. Opening five branches is not completing five branches. Confirm that Cultural victory is enabled before building a strategy around it.

## Learn with a compact, productive empire

A few high-quality cities, such as three mutually defensible cities with developed culture infrastructure, make this route easier to study. That number is a practice setup, not a hard rule. Compare the culture a new city will contribute with the policy-cost increase it creates.

Policy costs depend on adopted policies, non-puppet city count, map size, difficulty, speed and effects. A new city can affect costs before producing much culture, so aimless expansion often slows policy acquisition. A city that quickly develops culture and secures an important resource may still be worthwhile.

Before settling, inspect culture per turn, the remaining requirement for the next policy and how the new city will build its infrastructure. Higher total culture alone does not prove faster policies.

## Plan five compatible branches

One learning combination is **Tradition, Piety, Patronage, Commerce and Freedom**. They support early cities, culture and faith, city-state relations, finances and late great-person benefits. This is a compatible example rather than the only combination or a rigid adoption order.

| Stage | Decision | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ancient | Establish a development branch such as Tradition | Opening many branches for isolated rewards |
| Classical through Medieval | Order available benefits from Piety, Patronage and Commerce | Whether religion, city-states and gold actually support those choices |
| Industrial onward | Develop Freedom or another chosen late branch and finish the five-branch plan | Freedom, Order and Autocracy are mutually exclusive |

**Piety and Rationalism are mutually exclusive.** If you choose Rationalism to support research, revise the five-branch plan instead of also counting Piety's culture benefits. Choosing Piety does not remove the need for science: later culture buildings and military defense still require technologies.

## Research supports the culture-building chain

Develop [Monuments](/database/buildings/monument/) → [Amphitheaters](/database/buildings/amphitheater/) → [Opera Houses](/database/buildings/opera-house/) → [Museums](/database/buildings/museum/) → [Broadcast Towers](/database/buildings/broadcast-tower/) in the main cities. Relevant technologies include Drama and Poetry, Acoustics, Archaeology and Radio; follow their actual prerequisites.

The [Hermitage](/database/buildings/hermitage/) requires Opera Houses in all non-puppet cities. It supplies base culture and a **50% culture bonus** in this ruleset. Put it in a durable culture center and arrange timely Opera Houses elsewhere. Repeatedly founding weak production cities can postpone this milestone.

Do not race toward Radio while ignoring immediate defense or financial problems. Losing a city or stopping development during an invasion can cost more than an earlier culture-building unlock saves.

## Wonders are optional investments

Ask whether a wonder advances your current plan, whether production is available and how competitive the race appears. Suspending all defense and growth to pursue one wonder concentrates too much of the game in a single outcome.

Piety's Reformation provides a culture bonus in cities with a world wonder, making placement relevant. It does not require every city to enter an expensive wonder race. If another civilization wins a race, redirect production promptly to reliable culture, science or defense instead of immediately chasing another costly wonder.

## Great Artists: Landmarks or Golden Ages?

A [Great Artist](/database/units/great-artist/) can create a Landmark or start a Golden Age. An early Landmark can provide culture over many working turns; a late Golden Age can support a culture and production push. The Landmark uses a tile and a citizen's work, so its theoretical yield is not free of opportunity cost.

Freedom's completion effects on great improvements and Golden Age duration influence the choice. Compare expected usable benefits before the game ends, rather than always choosing one action. Keep the Artist safe and avoid relying on an improvement that will repeatedly be pillaged on an exposed border.

## The final stage needs production as well as culture

After completing five branches, assign the [Utopia Project](/database/buildings/utopia-project/) to a secure, strong production city. Its base cost is **1500 production**, with actual cost depending on game rules. It cannot be hurried; purchase or a Great Engineer should not be your completion plan.

As the fifth branch nears completion, finish unrelated long queues, prepare productive tile assignments and retain defenders. Do not reach the policy requirement only to discover that your project city is starving, besieged or occupied with avoidable work.

## Worked decision: more culture, slower policies

Suppose you add two small cities in the middle of the route. Total culture rises slightly, yet each policy takes longer. Check the increased cost, the new cities' building progress and whether they are delaying the Hermitage.

If their resources and positions justify keeping the expansion, prioritize their culture infrastructure. Pause further Settlers when the empire cannot develop them promptly. Do not respond by opening even more branches: count the policies still needed to complete the intended five.

This ruleset's route does not require tourism, great-work theming or artifact collection. Religion can support it; see the [religion guide](/strategies/religion/). Before ending a turn, check branch compatibility, completed buildings, Hermitage requirements and the final project's production and defense plan.
