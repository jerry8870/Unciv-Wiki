This guide targets **Gods & Kings, Standard speed, no mods, single-player Prince–King**. The situations below are constructed teaching exercises based on the rules, not played-game reports or verified win rates.

[Greece](/database/civilizations/greece/) maintains established city-state relationships efficiently. It does not gain free influence with every city-state each turn, and strong cavalry does not require annexing them. Compared with the [general diplomatic route](/strategies/diplomatic-victory/), spend the saved upkeep on useful network growth and protection while still watching competitors.

## Decay, recovery and friendly territory

| Ability or unique | Effect | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Influence decay | −50% degradation rate | Slower decline above the resting point, not +50% gift influence |
| Influence recovery | Twice normal recovery below the resting point | Stops at that point rather than automatically creating an ally |
| City-state territory | Always counts as friendly territory | Does not itself award an alliance, votes or immunity from war conditions |
| Hoplite | 13 strength versus Spearman's 11, retaining +50% versus Mounted | A front-line and anti-cavalry unit, not a guarantee of favorable terrain |
| Companion Cavalry | 14 strength / 5 movement versus Horseman's 12 / 4; requires Horses | Great Generals I, movement after attack, −33% city attack and no defensive terrain bonus |

Friendship starts at 30 influence. Alliance needs at least 60 **and** actual current ally status; reaching 60 does not exclude competitors. The default resting point is zero, but policies, protection and religion can change it. Check the individual city-state screen.

## Suitable conditions: an accessible network and an affordable core

Explore city-states with safe access, achievable quests and useful resources or positions. A small empire still needs income for troops, connections, research and gifts. Establish food, happiness and production before increasing diplomatic spending.

With many nearby city-states and little major-power pressure, expand through quests and worthwhile gifts. With few, distant or frequently conquered city-states, secure science, income and defense first. Whether diplomacy remains viable depends on actual votes, not the civilization label.

## Opening branches: complete useful quests

Record each city-state's quest, reward, location and competitors. Clearing a camp or connecting a resource already in your development plan may be worthwhile; radically changing research and construction for a single reward may not. Do not risk Workers, Settlers or the main army for an unsafe task.

[Hoplites](/database/units/hoplite/) defend the core and counter cavalry. [Companion Cavalry](/database/units/companion-cavalry/) can pursue, reinforce and handle flanks, subject to Horse supply and upkeep. Its −33% city attack means mobility does not replace a siege force. A war to protect a city-state needs a defined objective, not automatic annexation of the entire neighboring empire.

## Midgame: build room in the maintenance budget

In the Medieval era, consider [Patronage's](/mechanics/policies/) −25% influence decay opener. This adds percentage points to Greece's −50% for that modifier: without other effects, ordinary decay of 1 per turn becomes **0.25**. Shared religion, other civilizations' policies, personality and aggression toward city-states can change the result; 0.25 is not universal.

Gift influence depends on amount, game progress, speed, policies and quests. Use the current displayed award, comparing which gift restores a useful and maintainable alliance rather than following a fixed gold-conversion chart. A reserve for a crucial ally is often more useful than spreading all funds evenly across every contact.

Review your influence, the strongest competitor, current ally, decay, quest deadlines and military threats. Religion may help, but [beliefs](/mechanics/religion-beliefs/) have specific beneficiaries and conditions. An expensive founding program is not automatically justified by a small relationship benefit.

## Finish: relationships must survive until counting

Follow [diplomatic victory](/strategies/diplomatic-victory/) toward the United Nations and voting. Read the current threshold and separate stable votes, contested votes and votes you have not secured. A city-state vote has weight 1 and a major civilization's weight 2. Building the UN does not itself win, and maps do not all require the same total.

Do not stop planning at exactly enough current votes: competing gifts, wars and captured city-states can change the count. Protect decisive allies and plan one or two alternatives without counting unsecured city-states as guaranteed votes. The army's purpose is to preserve the core, allies and access so diplomatic investment can pay off.

## Teaching situation one: can 80 influence be left alone?

**Conditions:** An ordinary city-state has resting point zero and base decay 1, without religion, policy or other modifiers. Greece has 80 influence and is the current ally. Competitors take no new action.

**Choice:** Greece reduces decay to 0.5. After 20 turns it has 80 − 20 × 0.5 = **70**, versus 60 for an ordinary civilization in the same conditions. Use that room for another useful quest or economic investment rather than gifting every turn.

**Cost:** 70 is not guaranteed safety; a competing gift can change ally status immediately. Conquest can also remove the value of the relationship.

**Recheck:** Inspect competitors and actual ally status after quests, gifts and war events. Check more frequently near a vote and recalculate from actual decay and competition instead of relying only on a 20-turn countdown.

## Teaching situation two: negative recovery and an endangered vote

**Conditions:** At peace, influence is −10 with resting point zero and no other modifiers. Normal recovery is 1 per turn; Greece recovers 2. Another important city-state is under attack before an upcoming vote.

**Choice:** The first city-state can recover naturally to zero in five turns, then stops. It will not automatically reach 30 or 60. Direct urgent funding and troops toward preserving the second city-state; without a quest or gift plan, the recovering one is not a future ally you can count yet.

**Cost:** You delay improving the first relationship to prevent a decisive source of votes disappearing. Intervention can cost diplomacy and upkeep, so define the war's endpoint.

**Recheck:** After five turns confirm the resting point is reached; separately verify that the endangered city-state survives and remains allied. If war, protection or religion changes the resting point, recalculate this example.

## Recover a failing network

With insufficient income, stop gifts without a near-term purpose, complete development-compatible quests and repair the core economy. When an ally is taken, compare the competitive gap with the cost of an alternative vote. For a besieged ally, support a defense that can change the battle instead of spending every reserve on influence about to disappear. With too few city-states, reconsider the main victory route using the actual victory screen.

Use [terrain and promotions](/mechanics/combat-promotions/) for fighting and [Rome](/strategies/rome/) for the economic burden of conquest. Return to the [guide directory](/strategies/) for gaps in science or income.
