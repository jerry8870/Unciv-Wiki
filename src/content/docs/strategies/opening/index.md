Use this learning route for a **Gods & Kings game, Ancient start, Standard speed and no mods**. Prince or a lower difficulty is a useful starting point. Turn ranges organize the exercise; they are not deadlines. Terrain, ruins, civilization abilities and neighbors change the pace. Complete the purpose of a stage before moving on.

## Answer three questions before planning a build order

Where will your first citizens obtain useful food and production? Which luxury can support the next round of growth and expansion? From which direction can an enemy approach? These questions establish your population potential, expansion budget and defense needs.

When settling the capital, compare immediately usable tiles, fresh water, luxuries and defensive terrain. Moving for a clear, visible improvement can be reasonable. Wandering for several turns in search of an unknown perfect site delays everything. A mountain may eventually enable an [Observatory](/database/buildings/observatory/), but cannot substitute for an effective early economy.

## Around turns 1–15: establish information and useful production

Use the initial military unit to scout nearby city sites and barbarian approaches, then widen the search. Send a new [Scout](/database/units/scout/) farther away instead of following the same route. Explore a civilian's route before sending it into fog.

Try Scout → Monument → Worker as a learning queue, reconsidering it after each completion:

| Situation | Adjustment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Barbarians threaten the capital and your warrior cannot return quickly | Bring forward an Archer or available defender | An early Worker loses its value if captured |
| An immediately workable resource and its improvement technology are ready | Consider an earlier Worker | The improvement can start paying back promptly |
| There is little useful work or the technology is still missing | Build culture, food infrastructure or defense first | An idle, exposed Worker is not an economic advantage |
| Population is barely growing | Check citizens and net food before choosing a Granary | A building alone may not fix poor tile assignments |

Research follows the land. Prepare Mining for resources that need mines, Calendar for plantations, or Trapping for camps as appropriate. Follow the actual prerequisites. [Writing](/database/technologies/writing/) and [Libraries](/database/buildings/library/) matter, but should not automatically displace a technology that will immediately solve a food, happiness or security problem.

## Around turns 15–30: prepare a complete second-city plan

Choose the destination before producing a Settler. Give the city a specific job: obtain another luxury, protect a river approach, or claim strong workable tiles. Then check escort availability, barbarians along the route, happiness after settlement and whether a Worker can follow.

A nearby city is easier to reinforce and often cheaper to connect. A distant luxury may require extra troops and a long road. Compare tiles the city can use soon, rather than counting everything its eventual borders might contain.

For an initial policy direction, **Tradition** supports developing a capital and a few cities; **Liberty** supports expansion when land and the economy can sustain it. Liberty's free Settler and Worker help, but do not remove city costs. In a first practice game, concentrate on a branch long enough to understand its completion reward before scattering choices across several branches.

## Around turns 30–50: make the new city productive

Address food, cultural border growth, resource access and minimum defense first. A new city does not need to copy every capital building. Improve strong tiles that citizens can actually work; also account separately for the empire-wide value of connecting a luxury. Recheck assignments when population grows.

For peaceful development, plan Libraries and the [National College](/database/buildings/national-college/). The College requires Libraries in all non-puppet cities. A new low-production city can delay it. You can finish an expansion wave before completing Libraries, or finish the College before another wave. The tradeoff depends on competition for the land and the new city's ability to build its Library. Neither sequence is a universal rule.

Establish a defense plan: who blocks the approach, who deals ranged damage, and where wounded units retreat. A threatened city can switch to troops while the rear continues development. Pause expansion if repeated Settlers have exhausted happiness or money.

## Worked decision: what should the third build be?

This is a constructed teaching situation, not a recorded match. Your Scout and Monument are complete, a useful luxury lies west, a barbarian camp lies east, and your warrior is far away.

1. Determine whether the camp threatens a Worker's route. If so, build defense and bring the warrior within supporting distance.
2. Prepare the luxury's improvement technology so the eventual Worker will have a useful job.
3. Once the route is safe, build the Worker and investigate a second city to the west.
4. If that site borders a powerful neighbor, include its defense cost. Choose a safer nearby site if the extra commitment is unaffordable.

You delayed an economic build to preserve its value. Judge the decision by whether improvements and expansion resume safely, rather than by the earliest possible Worker completion.

## Recover when the opening slips

- **Growth stalls:** check net food, citizens and whether a short production push has become permanent.
- **Workers keep getting captured:** stop sending them through unexplored routes and establish a military screen.
- **The second city causes a deficit:** inspect roads, building and unit maintenance, and connections; see [expansion and economy](/strategies/expansion-economy/).
- **A neighbor attacks early:** pause wonders and Settlers, concentrate defenders, and avoid feeding individual reinforcements into enemy fire.

The stage is complete when each city has a useful project, key tiles are protected, you can explain why another city is worthwhile, and the next technology solves a real problem. Then choose a victory route from the [strategy hub](/strategies/).
