This guide covers **Gods & Kings with Diplomatic victory enabled**. Someone must build the United Nations, and you must win the diplomatic vote. Completing the wonder does not automatically win. Relationships and the situation when voting occurs determine whether your investment becomes support.

## Check that the map supports your plan

Explore major civilizations and city-states. Note which city-states are accessible, which quests fit your existing plans and who threatens their survival. A map with few city-states, or many being conquered, makes the usual alliance route harder. A large treasury alone does not establish a winning position.

Keep developing population, science and production. Research reaches [Globalization](/database/technologies/globalization/), production supports the UN or defense, and gold sustains critical relationships. Stopping research to hoard money can allow another victory before your plan is ready.

## Treat city-states as distinct investments

Maintain a small list of current ally, your influence, useful quests, geographic safety and relevant benefits. Prioritize city-states likely to survive, that you can support and whose quests can be completed alongside other goals.

| Opportunity | Useful response | Check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A nearby barbarian-clearing or similar quest | Combine it with scouting or defense | Whether the quest remains active and can be completed safely |
| A gold gift | Spend when influence can change a useful relationship | Actual influence gain and the present lead |
| Decaying influence with an ally | Complete a quest or add influence in time | Whether alliance status will survive until the vote |
| A city-state under attack | Evaluate defense, diplomacy and support routes | Cost and the risk of a wider war |

Friendship is not an alliance. A newly secured alliance is not permanent. Another civilization can gain influence, and your lead can decay. Recheck the city-state panel's actual ally after important actions.

## Patronage improves relationship maintenance

Patronage opens in the Medieval era and slows city-state influence decay. Its Philantropy policy increases influence from gold gifts. These effects improve the budget without eliminating the need for quests, funds and protection.

Consider whether you already have a useful network to maintain. If exploration is incomplete and development is in trouble, a policy that stabilizes the empire can be more urgent. A diplomatic strategy still needs a functioning economy and defenses.

Relations with major civilizations also matter, but friendly language is not a promise to vote for you. In the pinned AI logic, major-civilization voting considers diplomatic opinions among known major civilizations and can abstain in some situations; city-states choose their ally. Confirm actual support in the results rather than treating a prediction as a secured vote.

## The UN builder and the elected leader are separate

[Globalization](/database/technologies/globalization/) unlocks the [United Nations](/database/buildings/united-nations/), which starts diplomatic-victory voting. Anyone's completion satisfies the global building stage. One benefit of ownership in this version is that the owner's vote counts as **two votes**, while ordinary votes count as **one**.

If someone else is building it, prepare for elections even if your own construction queue contains no UN. If you build it, arrive at completion with relationships, cash and defenses ready, instead of exhausting the entire budget on development.

Election requires the game's minimum vote total and no tie for first place. The threshold depends on the available voting civilizations and UN state. It is **neither a fixed number of city-state allies nor always a simple-majority calculation**. Consult the displayed minimum and results. When information is incomplete, treat estimates as planning assumptions rather than confirmed victory.

## Prepare for a voting round

1. Recount surviving major civilizations and city-states instead of using an old estimate.
2. Inspect each important city-state's current ally and influence margin, marking contested relationships.
3. Complete suitable existing quests, then decide which gifts can change alliance status.
4. Keep a reserve for competition rather than spreading all cash across already secure allies.
5. Protect important allies from conquest and check your own core's safety.
6. Confirm your selection when voting and use the actual result to identify support and remaining gaps.

This is a management checklist, not a guarantee that every problem can be repaired on the final turn. Other civilizations continue acting, so establish a relationship margin early where possible.

## Worked decision: which city-state receives the next gift?

In this constructed budget exercise, ally A has a large influence lead, ally B has only just overtaken a competitor, and city-state C has a quest you may soon finish.

Verify that B is still your ally and whether C's quest can change its alliance. Do not give everything to A simply because it is already the friendliest. Build a margin at B if it is contested, retain some cash for changes, and do not count C as reliable support if the quest will miss the relevant window.

If support still falls well short of the threshold, strengthen the economy and find additional sources. War changes diplomatic relations, city-state survival, military costs and the threshold together; it is not a simple shortcut for reducing the required votes.

## Diagnose a failed election

Distinguish insufficient votes, a tie and an expected ally changing sides. Respond with additional support, a decisive contested vote or a more durable influence margin. If an ally disappeared through conquest, solve the survival problem instead of budgeting for a vote that no longer exists.

For chronic funding trouble, inspect [income and maintenance](/strategies/expansion-economy/). Continue monitoring other victory progress while waiting for another round; repeatedly buying back one city-state is not a complete long-term plan.
