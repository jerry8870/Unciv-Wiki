This guide targets **Gods & Kings, Standard speed, no mods, single-player Prince–King**. The situations below are constructed teaching exercises based on the rules, not played-game reports or verified win rates.

Each policy should answer two questions: what does the next choice solve, and when can the branch finish? A civilization's preferred victory is not an automatic policy order. Choose a main route in the [guide directory](/strategies/), then budget culture without counting benefits you have not earned.

## Check eras, prerequisites and exclusions

| Branch | Earliest era and important gates | Opening or completion benefits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tradition | Ancient; Landed Elite and Monarchy each require Legalism | Opener: +3 capital Culture, −25% natural border-growth culture cost empire-wide; completion: +15% growth empire-wide and free Aqueducts in the first four cities |
| Liberty | Ancient; Collective Rule requires Republic; Representation and Meritocracy require Citizenship | Opener: +1 Culture per city; completion: a free Great Person |
| Piety | Classical; Rationalism must not be adopted | Opener: +100% Production toward Shrines and Temples; completion: −20% Faith purchase cost in cities, +3 Gold and +3 Culture from Holy sites |
| Patronage | Medieval | Opener: −25% City-State Influence degradation; useful when quests, money and allies already form a plan |
| Rationalism | Renaissance; Piety must not be adopted | Opener: +15% Science while happy; completion: two free technologies and conditional Faith purchases of Great Scientists |
| Freedom / Order / Autocracy | Industrial; mutually exclusive | Emphasize specialists and great people, city development, or warfare respectively; compare individual policies before choosing |

Multiple policy prerequisites mean **all** are required. Reaching the era does not bypass them. A branch finishes after its ordinary policies are adopted; its completion reward does not require a separate culture purchase. Tradition and Liberty can coexist, but splitting between them delays finishers. Piety and Rationalism explicitly exclude one another here; do not import switching or reformation systems from another ruleset.

## Tradition or Liberty: write down the city plan

Tradition fits a strong existing capital and a limited near-term settlement plan. Legalism grants the cheapest culture building in the first four cities. Landed Elite adds 2 capital Food and 10% capital growth; Monarchy adds 1 Gold and 1 Happiness per 2 capital population. Compare the immediate bottleneck: food for a small city, or income and happiness for a larger one.

Liberty does not grant a Settler on opening. Republic precedes Collective Rule. Republic adds 1 Production per city and 5% building Production; Collective Rule gives the Settler and 50% capital Settler Production. Citizenship grants a Worker and faster improvements. Its successor Meritocracy includes happiness for capital-connected cities, which does not justify an expensive road network before the connections are useful.

Before committing, confirm scouted city sites, safe improvement access and room in the happiness budget. A free Settler without a viable site still needs protection and support. If neighbors have closed the frontier, do not force a settlement expedition solely to follow a wide-empire script.

## Piety or Rationalism: count actual beneficiaries

Piety suits a working Faith economy whose religious returns justify losing Rationalism. Organized Religion boosts Shrines and Temples. Reformation requires Organized Religion; Free Religion requires **both Mandate Of Heaven and Reformation**. Its policy-cost reduction is several choices away, not an opener benefit.

Rationalism suits population, Universities and staffed specialist slots. Secularism adds 2 Science per specialist. Free Thought requires Secularism; it adds 1 Science to Trading posts and 17 percentage points to University science bonuses. A [University's](/database/buildings/university/) own 33% and this 17% total 50% from these sources, rather than multiplying 33% by 1.17. Humanism precedes Sovereignty; Free Thought precedes Scientific Revolution. The two free technologies belong to the finisher.

## A four-step decision procedure

1. Name the bottleneck: food, happiness, workers, science, diplomacy funding or war.
2. Follow prerequisite arrows and count remaining choices, including any era wait.
3. Count cities, specialists and buildings that qualify now, excluding planned ones.
4. After adoption, inspect city or empire breakdowns; revisit the calculation after expansion, war or lost happiness.

Compare a distant finisher against the full cost of its path. A free Great Person is not enough reason to spend five poorly timed choices. Equally, a seemingly weaker immediate policy can be worthwhile when it completes a useful branch.

## Teaching situation one: three safe sites and a small capital

**Conditions:** Three defensible sites with new luxuries are scouted; the small capital lacks workers. Expansion is affordable and no strong army is approaching. No branch has been opened.

**Choice:** Consider Liberty → Republic → Collective Rule. If improvements are already behind, take Citizenship earlier so a Worker can connect resources around settlement time. The free Settler does not have to be consumed immediately.

**Cost:** You postpone Tradition's capital culture and population support. New cities need happiness, Libraries and defense. Republic's flat bonus totals +3 Production only after all three cities actually exist.

**Recheck:** After each settlement, inspect income, happiness, the next resource connection and the [National College's](/database/buildings/national-college/) Library requirement. If city two destabilizes the budget, delay city three and improve the existing economy.

## Teaching situation two: the Renaissance approaches, with weak religion

**Conditions:** Three cities have Universities and employ six specialists in total. Science is the main victory route. Neither Piety nor Rationalism is adopted, and religion has little reliable reach.

**Choice:** Plan Rationalism followed by Secularism. Six specialists gain 6 × 2 = **12 base Science per turn**, before each city's applicable modifiers. Follow prerequisites when considering Free Thought.

**Cost:** The opener's 15% requires happiness. Specialists displace tile workers; staffing six at the expense of growth or essential defense can undermine the gain.

**Recheck:** Inspect food surplus and science in every specialist city, especially after happiness falls. If Piety was already opened, this Rationalism branch is unavailable: realize existing religious benefits and build science infrastructure instead of budgeting inaccessible bonuses.

Growth percentages do not directly award population; use [citizens and improvements](/mechanics/resources-improvements/). Buildings do not staff specialists for you.
