## 1. Set up a manageable game

Choose the built-in Gods & Kings ruleset, an Ancient-era start and a generated map. Try a small map and a lower difficulty while learning. Keep the first game free of mods so the [database](/database/) matches what you see. Read each civilization's ability before choosing; [Rome](/database/civilizations/rome/) offers an example of an ability linked to city development.

On iPhone or iPad, follow the [iOS installation guide](/ios/). Check the build actually offered in TestFlight. Achievement eligibility is stricter than an ordinary learning game: [read the requirements](/achievements/) before starting if medals are your goal.

## 2. Found the capital and inspect its tiles

Use your Settler to found a city after checking food, production and nearby resources. Avoid spending many turns searching for a perfect location in a first game. Open the city screen and inspect which tiles citizens work. Food supports growth; production completes units and buildings. A worked tile matters immediately, while an unworked improvement may be an investment for later.

A simple opening to consider is a Scout, a Monument and then a Worker or defensive unit, adjusted for nearby danger. A Worker improves tiles; it does not found cities. Compare the [Settler](/database/units/settler/) and [Worker](/database/units/worker/) entries if their roles are unclear.

## 3. Scout, then choose useful research

Explore in more than one direction, keep vulnerable units away from hostile units and reveal the approaches to your capital. Do not send every defender far away. Research a technology that unlocks something you can use: a needed improvement, protection or a growth building.

For example, [Writing](/database/technologies/writing/) leads to the [Library](/database/buildings/library/). The database's cost is the ruleset base, so the price in your current game can differ. Follow prerequisite and unlock links rather than treating the tree as a list of unrelated names.

## 4. Grow without losing control

Each turn, check happiness, gold income, city production and exposed units. Improve resources your citizens can work. Protect a new Settler, and expand when you can defend and support the next city. More cities create opportunities and costs; a first game is easier to understand with a few purposeful cities than with uncontrolled expansion.

When combat begins, compare strength, terrain and the attack preview. Ranged units soften a target; a suitable capturing unit is needed to take a city. Heal and reposition instead of assuming a damaged unit can survive another attack.

## 5. Choose a direction and review each turn

After meeting neighbours and learning your surroundings, choose a victory direction supported by the current game. Keep science progressing, read diplomacy offers before accepting and learn one new system at a time. Religious play can wait until the basic city and turn loop makes sense.

Before ending a turn, ask: are cities producing something useful, is income sustainable, are vulnerable units protected, and does the next research choice support the plan? If a result surprises you, check the relevant [unit](/database/units/), [building](/database/buildings/) or [mechanics](/mechanics/) entry.

## If you are also collecting achievements

Start an eligible new game and follow each medal's starting difficulty and opponent requirements. Do not assume a first learning match qualifies for every tier. Use the [achievement troubleshooting guide](/guides/achievement-troubleshooting/) when a condition appears complete but the medal is still locked.


## Continue through a complete match

This page is an orientation. Practice conditional builds and research with [the first 50 turns](/strategies/opening/), stabilize cities with [expansion and economy](/strategies/expansion-economy/), then select science, culture, domination or diplomacy in the [detailed match guides](/strategies/).
